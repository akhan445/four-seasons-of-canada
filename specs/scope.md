# Four Seasons of Canada

An interactive, storybook-style website that teaches the four seasons through a map of Canada and what is unique to the country in each one.

## The Unique Kernel
The whole page transforms to *feel* like the season you tap. In autumn the page turns red, orange and yellow and maple leaves drift across it. The same map of Canada then shows only what is special to that season: animals, plants, and natural events like the northern lights.

## Who It's For
Anyone from young kids to adults. Kids should be delighted by it, and adults should appreciate the art. It is an educational tool for learning about Canada, its seasons and its geography in an interactive way.

## The Core Loop
Open the page and see one big map of Canada. Tap a season tab and the theme changes. Explore the map, hover over a pinpoint, and read a card about something unique to that season and place. Switch seasons and discover a different set of things.

## Inspiration & Identity
An animated storybook / children's coloring-book feel. Playful and happy for kids, with enough craft that adults appreciate the look. Each season has its own colors and animated touches (for example, falling maple leaves in autumn).

## Why This Matters to the Learner
Mainly a way to learn the process of building a project from end to end, and to test out this skill pack. It is also a project the learner finds fun and meaningful: teaching Canada, and Toronto in particular, in an interactive way.

## What "Working" Looks Like
- One page with a map of Canada and four season tabs.
- Tapping a season changes the whole page's theme and shows animation (the "oh, that's cool" beat: autumn leaves flying across a red/orange/yellow page).
- Each season shows at least 5 pinpoints on the map of things unique to Canada in that season (animals like the moose, beaver, or polar bear; plants; natural events like the northern lights or the long days of northern summer).
- Hovering over a pin shows a card connected to the pin by a dotted line. The card is a boxed, outlined panel with a picture and a short paragraph. If a pin has several things, the card works like a small gallery with a "next" button.

## The POC Boundary
- Single page, single map of the whole country.
- Four season tabs with theme and animation changes.
- At least 5 meaningful pinpoints per season (20 or more in total), each with a hover card (dotted line, outlined box, picture, short paragraph, and next button for multiple items).

## Later
- Clicking a province to zoom in and see more detailed pinpoints for that province (for example, Ontario and Toronto).
- More pinpoints per season.

## Explicitly Cut
- Province zoom for the first version. The learner chose a whole-country map to keep the first version small. It moves to Later.
